## 2. New independent AURA findings

### N1 — OmniAct: morphology-conditioned control (highest transfer relevance)
[arXiv 2611.33158v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33158v2), Nov 24 v1, Nov 25 v2; [project](https://omniact.github.io/) and [repository](https://github.com/sfanxiang/omniact) checked Nov 26. A graph locates robot joints/fingertips in camera frames and represents their motion axes and connectivity. A flow policy outputs relative joint motion per node instead of expecting one fixed action vector. Training uses simulated trajectories on one million procedurally filtered embodiments, a very large undertaking despite a 0.4B policy.

Authors report 63.0% on 2,000 held-out embodiment/scene episodes vs 57.9% strongest comparator and 17.8% pi0.5. Physical pick/place: OpenYAM 13/20 vs MolmoAct2 7/20; Franka 14/20 vs DreamZero 15/20. Unsupported embodiments are labelled N/S in the project table, not successful transfer tests. The paper/project are accessible; the linked repo on master contains README/assets and explicitly lists code, checkpoints and generator as pending. Not a downloadable policy today.

Why AURA cares: hardware-aware behavior is different from action-vector conversion. Compare capability retention when reach, joint limits or gripper geometry changes. A low-cost future reference would document one known robot's camera-relative kinematic graph, validate frame/unit transformations and compare geometry-aware versus geometry-blind control under matched tasks. No million-robot training or hardware purchase. DEEP DIVE / WATCH release; MONITOR/BACKLOG.

### N2 — Screw Attention Policy: supply geometry, learn execution
[arXiv 2611.32797v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32797v1), Nov 24; [HTML §§II–III and Tables II–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.32797v1) inspected. A task-level language/pose estimation stack produces geometric relations, then a 30K-trainable-parameter controller uses SE(3)/screw representations and an action-primitive estimator to predict end-effector twists. Authors report LIBERO 96.0% (estimated pose), layout shift 85.8%, five unseen arms 80.2%, real robot 21/24.

Crucial qualification: 30K is NOT total system size. Frozen language/perception models, object meshes available in the simulator, step parsing and an operational-space executor matter. Table IV position-swap/recovery ablations use ground-truth poses; 76% missed-grasp and 56% dropped-object recovery therefore do not establish nonprivileged recovery. Standard estimated-pose results and true-pose diagnostics are distinct. Cross-arm comparisons use published benchmark baselines; no matched end-to-end training/latency audit here. Code/weights not confirmed.

This is a direct alternative to high-scale VLA or a large external verifier. Does geometric feedback alone already deliver recovery? Later compare frozen detector/pose pipeline plus simple controller, compact learned geometry controller and sensor-limited baseline; log mesh/pose assumptions, calibration errors, target gap and full-stack compute. DEEP DIVE / MONITOR.

### N3 — Movement Prototype Quantization (MPQ): isolate modular skill costs
[arXiv 2611.32858v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32858v1), Nov 24; [project](https://intuitive-robots.github.io/mpq/) read. Rather than jointly learning a codebook, selection and decoding, authors map an already-trained policy's action chunk to the nearest demonstrated motion and apply bounded correction. No parameter training for this wrapper. Across six architectures they report statistical equivalence to the original policy under their evaluation. On LIBERO-90, selector errors contribute at most 2 points when reference placement/correction are supplied; learned placement/correction explain most remaining gap (up to 12 points). Offline reconstruction fails to predict these closed-loop effects.

AURA cares because it separates library coverage, selection and execution: a modular interface is not inherently a useful skill abstraction. Test concept on a compatible frozen policy and existing trajectory corpus only after approval; compare original chunks, nearest prototypes, bounded correction, and matched selector substitutions. Track action frame/time semantics, healthy-case preservation and full latency. Code/checkpoint availability not verified. DEEP DIVE; candidate principle for WP3, MONITOR (not an additional experiment nomination).

### N4 — Keypose or Dense? Action abstractions trade one sensitivity for another
[arXiv 2611.32788v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32788v1), Nov 24, [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.32788v1) checked. Stage-endpoint targets plus speed-limited servo are compared against step-by-step targets with matched demonstrations, observations, architectures and update budgets. Authors find keyposes less sensitive to distorted demonstration timing and some object shifts, but not universally superior with clean data. On real drawer opening, dense wins initially; changing keypose servo behavior improves the latter.

AURA implication: transferring a policy can relocate the bottleneck to executor timing, orientation and contact. The meaningful control is action representation AND matched compatible execution, not "keyposes are better." Document robot/controller assumptions and test source/target timing perturbations later. Assets unverified. DEEP DIVE / MONITOR.

### N5 — iWAM: future conditioning without rendering a future
[arXiv 2611.33141v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33141v1), Nov 24; [HTML §§4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.33141v1) checked. Invert demonstrated futures to latent noise during training; learn a bidirectional flow shortcut. One video-backbone pass at inference yields future-conditioned features for the action model. Authors show their FastWAM-style no-future comparator is weak on shifted DROID tasks (9.2% simulated, 13.3% real vs full IDM 96.7%, 80.0%). Their iWAM(MIP) has 91.7% simulated success vs 96.7% IDM, with about 6x lower inference latency in their timing test. Important: A100 80GB, batch 1; baseline timing uses two action-denoising steps while its reported success uses eight, so these are not matched measured operating points. Wan2.2-5B backbone + 1B action expert; not a cheap model training proposal. Artifacts not audited.

This challenges blanket removal of prediction: compressing predictive information may be better than discarding it, particularly when the target environment differs. Compare no future, training-only features, implicit future conditioning, value conditioning and explicit futures at a common deployment budget and target shift. No WAM install/training now. DEEP DIVE / MONITOR.

### N6 — RoC and Embodied-Harness: important prior-art pressure
[Reasoning on Call, 2611.26341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26341), first submission Nov 19 (UTC); [Embodied-Harness, 2611.27689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27689), Nov 20; source records and selected text inspected. RoC uses temporal monitoring plus ordered memory to call slower reasoning only at events, repair policy execution/plan and update memory after outcome checking. Authors report RMBench +24.0 success-rate points and 65.3% fewer tokens vs chosen agent comparator, +15.1 points vs policy comparator. Token consumption is not wall-clock latency.

Embodied-Harness uses actor/reflector agents, short/long-term memory and evidence-guided operational advice revision without changing model weights. Author results 82.4%, 76.7%, 94.4% on RoboTwin 2.0, RoboSPA, RMBench respectively and 96.7% average on three real tasks. Artifact availability and strictly isolated related/unrelated prior transfer not established by this check. Both overlap with AURA's event monitoring, recovery and experience learning. Later comparison should hold policy/API, information privileges, intervention budget and world resets fixed; include no-reflection and no-extra-memory controls. DEEP DIVE / MONITOR.

### N7 — Data/experience and Real2Sim
- [RoboRecast 2611.32622v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32622v1), Nov 24: diagnose simulated retargeting failures and adjust scene/motion parameters to preserve the demonstrated interaction under object/layout/embodiment shift. Author claims benefit on single-arm real and bimanual sim tasks; code and precise adaptation budget unverified. AURA should record whether geometry/task semantics are preserved and whether changing a target scene makes evaluation artificially easier. Small later control: raw retargeting vs constraint-preserving revision, held-out targets fixed before augmentation. READ → DEEP DIVE / MONITOR.
- [MXI-MemICL 2611.32564v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32564v1), Nov 24: encode action effects, retrieve memories by predicted next-stage intent and gate their use in a VLA. Authors report gains on RoboCasa365/RoboTwin and real tasks; code promised, not confirmed. No parameter update during in-context use does not mean no trained modules. Compare related vs shuffled/unrelated experiences and stale retrieval, at equal context budget. READ / WATCH.
- [Support2Sim 2611.32727v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32727v1), Nov 24: RGB-to-simulation support graph plus contact-constrained pose search. Authors report 87.1% vs 73.2% support realization across 50 scenes, lower drift (45.3 vs 79.6 mm). Not a manipulation-success result. Useful to distinguish visually matched scenes from physically valid transferred setups; future check support/contact/settling before policy evaluation. READ / WATCH artifacts.
- [ROAM-Bench 2611.33143v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33143v1), Nov 24: 250 manually validated active-spatial tasks; independent state/episode checks separate right answers from fulfilled physical requirements. Navigation-heavy scope partly outside AURA; evaluator-only simulator truth is acceptable, policy truth leakage is not. READ evaluation principle / MONITOR, no navigation branch.

## 4. Decisions

Stable goal, contestable mechanisms. OmniAct supplies robot kinematics to a learned policy; Screw Attention supplies task geometry to compact execution; MPQ and Keypose/Dense test skill/action boundary failure. None establishes a universal interface or proves that all modules are interchangeable. A transfer claim needs source/target, allowable adaptation, target-held-out success and full stack resources. iWAM is a material refinement of "no explicit online video necessary": future-specific information may still matter, so compare utility under distribution shift, not just training or inference cost. RoC/Embodied-Harness strengthen prior-art pressure on a generic agent wrapper; a smaller controller or integrated policy may win. Long-horizon structure/history, bounded prediction and learning from recovery remain hypotheses, not required separate modules.

[Handoff](2026-11-26-handoff.md) extends WP1–WP4; no assignments. The existing proposed post-M9a freshness/state-invalidation question remains the next checkpoint candidate (null events and persistent calibration bias). No additional study nominated. Transfer-oriented action-interface comparisons are later alternatives. Feishu pilot is a separate workbench owner; no live import/sync or platform claim here.
