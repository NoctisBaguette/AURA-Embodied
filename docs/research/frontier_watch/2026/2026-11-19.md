## 2. InteractVLA — frozen policies become steerable tools (NEW, Nov 17)

**Primary:** [arXiv:2611.22584 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22584); [project page](https://lingwupro.github.io/InteractVLA/); [official repo](https://github.com/LingwuPro/InteractVLA). Its README was inspected on Nov 19 at blob **b025ab3ef18260f718215acb1cbe1d05dbd02649**; it links actual PyTorch/JAX guidance, geometric objectives, feedback/runtime and tests. **No local installation performed.**

**What happened / capability:** A high-level agent specifies an end-effector pose; differentiable command-to-pose guidance steers a **frozen flow-based VLA** at sampling time. Soft-time target alignment avoids forcing the intended pose at a rigid action index. The returned action chunk and calibrated camera projection let the agent inspect, revise and retry the target. Compatibility checks may decline a target. This is a **bidirectional geometric policy interface**, not merely natural-language API wrapping.

**Evidence / limits:** authors claim **99.4% LIBERO-Plus, 48.62% RoboDojo, 89.4% RoboCasa365**; the repository itself flags nonidentical protocols and agent backends among comparisons. A pose compatibility score is not calibrated task success, and similar percentages are not matched policy/wall-clock/privilege evidence. Geometry, differentiable action expert, coordinate normalization and gripper semantics are hard compatibility requirements.

**AURA relevance and possible check:** directly relevant to reusable VLA backends and scene migration without full retraining. **WP3 source-only** can inspect supported inputs, output units, world/base/end-effector frames, action chunk cadence, camera calibration, model backends and actual local hardware cost. An eventually approved ablation would compare **same frozen policy** with (a) raw actions, (b) pose guidance and (c) inspection/refinement, measuring physical success, harmful overrides, end-to-end latency, agent/API calls and shifted layouts. **DEEP DIVE; MONITOR/BACKLOG**. No M9a replacement.

## 3. CoBoVLA — small, interpretable command/action codes (NEW, Nov 17)

**Primary:** [arXiv:2611.22395 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22395). Decouples natural language into **step codes**, visually grounded planning into **action codes**, and executes through a language-independent actor. Total **250M parameters**. Authors claim **91.4% LIBERO Goal**, **81.1% LIBERO-Para**, about **17ms/control step** under their hardware; neither code/checkpoint access nor AURA-device latency was verified.

**Why this matters:** cheap deployment and interpretable failures might be more attainable than very large policy pretraining. Language robustness is not automatically unseen-object, camera or robot transfer. **Possible test:** under equal data/latency and a held-out language/scene matrix, compare compact baseline, code factorization and trivial instruction canonicalization. Check whether errors stem from goal parsing or control, not from memorized paraphrase templates. **DEEP DIVE / WATCH artifacts; MONITOR/BACKLOG**.

## 4. MARS — learned atomic skill routing (NEW, Nov 14)

**Primary:** [arXiv:2611.19324 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19324). Routes a frozen general policy to stage-specific RL atomic skills through recurrent and semantic stage inference. Authors report **90.47% macro-stage F1** on evaluated trajectory labels and selected simulated tube-transfer success **20.04%→85.67%**. Training specialist skills and labeling stage boundaries have real cost. A router can make dangerous switches under sensor shift.

**AURA relevance:** compare alternative integrated policies, explicit skills and simple rule-based stage selection, measuring handoff failures, training/demo cost, stage latency and held-out scene behavior. Interface modularity does not prove learned modules are independently interchangeable. **READ→DEEP DIVE; BACKLOG**.

## 5. ThinkWAM — action-conditioned online prediction (NEW, Nov 16)

**Primary:** [arXiv:2611.21258 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21258). A **propose–rethink–refine** action loop uses imagined action-conditioned futures to modify action chunks at inference. Authors report roughly **153–165ms** generation in selected sim settings and task-dependent performance; AURA has not measured total robot-serving latency.

**Why it matters:** compare actual **runtime predictor** against training-only predictive supervision from JAM/PDRVLA/DreamFree/ReLAP, a cheap state verifier, and no predictor. Inference-time futures may aid reorientation but cost time and mislead precision contact. **Possible experiment after approval:** matched policy, episode seeds, data, wall-time and disturbance conditions; report physical effect correctness, false interventions and all inference costs. **DEEP DIVE; BACKLOG**.

## 6. Task Convergent Flow Matching — task progress rather than plausible motion (NEW, Nov 17)

**Primary:** [arXiv:2611.22609 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22609). Scores candidate action chunks using parallel **physics rollouts** and **task-progress funnels**; bounded temporary regressions are allowed while net progress is required. CMA-ES refines sampled flow seeds under prior-distribution constraints. Authors report **PushT success 31.3%→88.5%** and three-cube stacking **77%→86%**.

**AURA value:** strong **evaluation principle** separating actual physical transition from controller completion, temporary motion and stale state. **Limitation:** uses physics simulation and privileged progress measures to select actions, not a nonprivileged camera-only verifier. Future offline comparator: physical-effect truth for scoring only; online verifier must not access reference state. Track stall/false completion/regression and whole-query search time. **DEEP DIVE; BACKLOG**.

## 7. ReLAP and PriDex — data and embodiment transfer (NEW, Nov 16–17)

**ReLAP:** [arXiv:2611.22077 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22077), Nov 17. Recurrent action-relevant latent features pretrained from videos without action labels; prediction head need not run at deployment. Authors report **97.9% LIBERO, 70.0% LIBERO-Plus**; large data/compute remain, runnable weights not independently checked. Challenges compulsory online WAM; candidate for **READ→DEEP DIVE, BACKLOG**, not a training launch.

**PriDex:** [arXiv:2611.21908 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21908), Nov 16. Human hand/object-relative motion priors with confidence-weighted retargeting and subsequent simulation RL for selected dual-arm tasks. Cross-embodiment interface quality and real physical adaptation are the research questions; not instant zero-shot transfer across all robots. **READ / WATCH; BACKLOG**. Future measures: target-domain demos, calibration/retargeting effort, physics divergence and held-out task success.

## 8. JointGV and ScaleVLA — artifact and compute boundaries (NEW, Nov 16–17)

**JointGV:** [arXiv:2611.22543 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22543), Nov 17. Graph-visual residual prediction lowers reported long-horizon state error in assorted controls, not a broad manipulation result. Its advertised [GitHub repository](https://github.com/JasonLvernex/Joint_Graph_Vedio_Latent_Dynamic_World_Model) returned **404** when checked; **do not claim code availability**. **READ** if bounded dynamics prediction becomes a specific problem; no reproduction.

**ScaleVLA:** [arXiv:2611.21607 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21607), Nov 16, [official repo](https://github.com/GalaxyGeneralRobotics/ScaleVLA). Reuses an enormous **552B frozen backbone** with an action expert; author reports gains in low-demo and shift settings. This is a meaningful *large-model benchmark for transfer*, not a feasible AURA training path without an explicit infrastructure budget. **READ/WATCH; BACKLOG**.

## 9. Upstream findings independently checked for AURA, not re-discovered

**PEAR — native robot-use RL:** [paper Nov13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562), [official repo](https://github.com/mit-han-lab/pear), README blob **677d94fa2ba75cfcb06127032320a5378cea15a3** inspected Nov19. A 27B reasoning model trained with simulation-only robot-use RL learns pose-commanding behavior. Authors report **LIBERO 1.6→74.4%** and selected real dual-arm **60%**. The repository actually documents veRL/Ray/GRPO plus **H100/H200/GB300 training**, model serving via RTX5090/GB300. It still depends on ordinary robot controllers: “no bespoke reasoning harness” is **not no control stack**. **DEEP DIVE** competing end-to-end learning; NO ACTION on full training.

**Reins — guarded execution with concurrent observation:** [paper Nov12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16632). Conditioned atomic actions execute only while preconditions hold; a separate narrator refreshes observations concurrently. Authors report **88% across five real dual-arm tasks** under a frontier model. Code release was not confirmed. This tests whether a fixed agent benefits from fresher evidence and guarded tools **without retraining the policy**; measure false stops, stale-state errors, tool/agent calls and elapsed time. **DEEP DIVE / code WATCH**.

**Agentic-Teacher — failure-driven VLA training:** [paper Nov12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17394). Generates validated continuations from states where the original VLA failed, filters/noises them and updates the VLA. The coding agent need not execute at runtime. Authors report **LIBERO-10 79.4→87.8%**, selected RoboCasa **0→30%**. Simulation and tool/expert assumptions prevent universal transfer claims; complete training pipeline unconfirmed. Equal-data random episodes vs failure-localized continuations is a future falsifiable comparison. **DEEP DIVE; BACKLOG**.

**STVLA — geometry-aware temporal memory:** [paper Nov12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16589), relevant to occlusion and viewpoint shifts; selected reported real gains, but extra pretrained geometry features confound a claim of memory-selection benefit. Compare appearance-only, geometry-only, and combined memory at equal latency/memory. **READ→DEEP DIVE; BACKLOG**.

**Proprioceptive anchor / persistent calibration bias:** [paper Nov13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17658) shows open-loop sensitivity to position noise can understate **closed-loop sensitivity to sustained offsets**; all **15 qualifying** simulated policies show this contrast under the author's gates. This is synthetic-noise simulation, not a camera hardware test or proof M9a is invalid. A **cheap negative control** for later transfer/evidence validity studies is **random frame noise vs persistent geometric offset**, measuring both action sensitivity and closed-loop physical success. **READ→DEEP DIVE; BACKLOG**.

## 10. Architecture and decision implications

AURA's [ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/NoctisBaguette/AURA-Embodied/blob/main/ARCHITECTURE.md) already treats models as replaceable **capabilities and software interfaces**, not mandatory separable trained modules. Compare (A) frozen VLA + geometric steering, (B) compact symbolic/latent task-control codes, (C) integrated robot-use policy, (D) guarded external tool execution, (E) training-only predictive guidance and (F) online physics prediction. No universal winner is demonstrated. DynaHarness, RoboArchitect and Reins narrow generic “planner+memory+verification+recovery” novelty; **DynaHarness primary evidence was not re-audited this week** and is retained solely as a previously identified comparator.

Transfer means a pinned **source vs target** object/task/layout/camera/robot/action-frame/data split, permitted adaptations, new demonstrations and compute budget, target success and failure analysis. Adapter API compatibility and smaller checkpoints alone are **not** scientific transfer evidence.
